# Transcription and Auditory Priming Notes

Low-quality speech is vulnerable to **caption-induced auditory priming** and **auditory
pareidolia**. When the acoustic signal is incomplete, visible words give the brain a
candidate phonetic template. The listener may then experience the suggested phrase as
clearer than the recording independently warrants.

This does not establish intentional deception by an analyst. A common sequence is:

1. the analyst repeatedly listens and forms an interpretation;
2. the interpretation is placed in captions;
3. later listeners read the caption before hearing the clip;
4. the caption becomes difficult to “unhear.”

For this material, captions claiming the name “Nolan” should not be treated as validation.
The name was not independently apparent during the uncued review documented here.

## Suggested reporting language

Use:

> The isolated channel contains faint speech-like audio. One passage is plausibly heard
> as “shut the fuck up.” A later passage more strongly resembles “oh my God,” followed
> by a possible “what did/do we do.” No intelligible use of the name “Nolan” was
> confirmed. All proposed wording remains subject to the limitations of a compressed
> YouTube-derived source.

Avoid:

- “The recording definitely says…”
- “The enhancement proved…”
- treating captions as independent corroboration;
- relying on an aggressive residual without presenting the raw clip.
